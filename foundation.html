2 Professional training video

After you watch — Restaurant Reflection: What is one thing from this video your team must remember on Opening Day?
3 What I need to know
Visual · The Customer Trust Chain
🧑Me→🍽Food→🤗Customer→⭐Trust
✅ Must know
  • Customers trust you that the food is safe, the kitchen is clean, and you have been honest about what is in it.
  • Unsafe food can make people ill — being sick, tummy aches, missing school.
  • You cannot always see or smell what makes food unsafe, so you follow the safe rules every time.
  • Food safety is everyone’s job on the team, not just the cook’s.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Serving food just because it “looks fine”.
  • Taking shortcuts when the restaurant gets busy.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The team agrees its safety rules before opening and follows them every time.
  • Anyone on the team can call “stop” if they see something unsafe.

3 What I need to know
Visual · Ready to Cook Checklist
🧼Clean hands
🨍Clean apron
👳Hair tied back
🩹Cuts covered — blue plaster
💍No jewellery
📱Phone away
✅ Must know
  • Ready to cook = clean hands, clean apron, hair tied back, cuts covered with a blue plaster, no jewellery, phone away.
  • We use a blue plaster because blue is easy to spot if it falls into food.
  • If you feel ill — a tummy bug, being sick, or a bad cold — tell an adult and do not handle food.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Cooking with loose hair, a ring on, or an uncovered cut.
  • Touching your phone and then food.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The team does a “ready to cook” check together before starting.
  • One person keeps spare aprons and blue plasters at the station.

Visual · The Invisible Journey

✅ Must know
  • Germs are too small to see and spread easily on hands.
  • Wash for about 20 seconds with soap — scrub palms, backs, between fingers and under nails.
  • Wash before handling food, after the toilet, after touching raw meat, and after coughing or touching your face.
✗ Common mistakes
  • A quick splash of water with no soap.
  • Forgetting to wash between different jobs.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The team agrees its “wash times” that everyone follows.
  • Soap and clean towels are always stocked at the station before service.

Control the Kitchen

“Can I keep foods apart safely?”
🧠 Smart about Thinking
1 The situation
Someone cuts raw chicken on a board, then starts cutting salad for a customer on the same board.
2 Professional training video
Before you watch: Watch for how germs travel from one food to another.
▶Watch: Avoiding cross-contaminationopens in a new tab
While you watch, look for:
  • how germs move from raw food
  • why we keep raw and ready-to-eat food apart
  • how to use chopping boards safely
After you watch — Restaurant Reflection: Where in your team’s menu could raw and ready-to-eat food meet?
3 What I need to know
Visual · The Contamination Chain
🦡Sourceraw meat→✋Transferhands & boards→🍳Food→🤗Customer
✅ Must know
  • Cross-contamination is when germs move from one food to another.
  • Germs come from raw meat, fish and eggs.
  • Keep raw and ready-to-eat food apart: separate chopping boards, wash hands in between, clean surfaces and equipment, and store raw food at the bottom of the fridge.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Using the same board for raw chicken and then salad.
  • Not washing hands after touching raw meat.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The team plans which boards and utensils are for raw food and which are for ready-to-eat.
  • Surfaces are cleaned between different jobs.

Protect the Ingredients

“Can I keep food safe until service?”
🧠 Smart about Situations

2 Professional training video
Before you watch: Watch for what happens to food when it is kept warm.
▶Watch: Storing food safelyopens in a new tab▶Watch: Holding food at safe temperaturesopens in a new tab
While you watch, look for:
  • why temperature matters
  • where different foods should be kept
  • what happens when food is left out
After you watch — Restaurant Reflection: Which food on your team’s menu would be most at risk if it was left out?
3 What I need to know
Visual · The Temperature Danger Zone
Hot & safe — above 63°Ckeep hot food hot
DANGER ZONE — 5°C to 63°Cgerms grow fast — don’t leave food here
Cold & safe — below 5°Ckeep cold food in the fridge
Safe Storage
✅ Top shelves: ready-to-eat food — cooked food, salad, desserts
⚠️ Bottom shelf: raw meat, fish and eggs
✅ Must know
  • Germs grow fast when food is warm — keep cold food cold (in the fridge) and hot food hot.
  • Do not leave food sitting out — put it away until it is needed.
  • Cover food to keep it clean, and store raw food at the bottom of the fridge.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Leaving food out on the counter, especially somewhere warm.
  • Leaving prepared food uncovered.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The team decides where each food is stored, and keeps it covered and labelled.
  • Someone checks that food is put away after it is prepared.

Serve Everyone Safely

“Can every customer trust me?”
🧠 Smart about People
1 The situation
A customer points at your cupcakes and asks, “Do these have nuts in them? I’m allergic.” You helped make them, but you are not totally sure what went in.
2 Professional training video

While you watch, look for:
  • what an allergy is

After you watch — Restaurant Reflection: How will your team make sure your allergen information is always correct?
3 What I need to know
Visual · The Allergen Safety System
🗣️Customer
asks→📋Check the
Allergen Card→🤷Not sure?ask an adult→✅Tell the
customer
MilkEggsFishShellfishPeanutsTree nutsWheatSoyaSesame
✅ Must know
  • An allergy can make someone very ill — sometimes from even a tiny amount.
  • Foods people are often allergic to include milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, peanuts, other nuts, wheat, soya and sesame.
  • The team keeps an Allergen Card; check it, and if you are not sure say “Let me check for you” and ask an adult.
  • No peanuts or tree nuts are used anywhere in the food court.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Saying “I think it’s fine” — guessing about an allergy.
  • Answering from memory instead of checking the Allergen Card.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The team writes and checks an Allergen Card for every dish.
  • Everyone knows where the Allergen Card is and how to use it.

Pass the Inspection

“Is my restaurant ready to open?”
🧠 Smart about Situations
1 The situation
It is the busiest moment of opening day. There is a spill on the floor, the bin is full, and orders are coming in fast.
2 Professional training video
Before you watch: Watch for how professionals keep a kitchen clean during a rush.
▶Watch: Cleaning & sanitising equipmentopens in a new tab▶Watch: Keeping the kitchen cleanopens in a new tab

3 What I need to know
Visual · The Opening Inspection Cycle
🧹Prepare→🔍Check→🍽Serve→📝Review↻
✅ Must know
  • Clean as you go — wipe surfaces between jobs and clean spills straight away.
  • Watch for hazards: wet floors are slippery, pans get hot, and knives are carried point-down.
  • Keep the bin tidy and away from food, and do a full clean after service.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Leaving a spill “for later” — someone could slip.
  • Letting mess build up during the rush.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The team has a cleaning plan — who cleans, and when.
  • Hazards like spills are dealt with straight away.
