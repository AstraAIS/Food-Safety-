Protect the Customer

“How does my team build systems customers can trust?”
🧠 Smart about People
1 The situation
Your stall is about to open. Three different people on your team have handled the food today, and a customer is asking whether it’s safe. How do you make sure the answer is always yes — no matter who was on shift?
2 Professional training video
Before you watch: As you watch, can you spot what a customer is really trusting a restaurant to do?

While you watch, look for:
  • why food safety matters
  • what customers are trusting you to do
  • that food safety is everyone’s job
After you watch — Restaurant Reflection: What is one thing from this video your team must remember on Opening Day?
3 What I need to know
Visual · The Customer Trust Chain
🧑Me→🍽Food→🤗Customer→⭐Trust
✅ Must know
  • Customers trust you that food is safe, the kitchen is clean, and you have been honest about what is in it.
  • Unsafe food causes real illness — and one mistake by one person affects the whole team’s reputation.
  • Trust does not come from one careful person; it comes from a system everyone follows the same way.
  • If a system depends on memory, it fails when the team is busy — so good restaurants write it down.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Relying on the most careful person instead of a shared routine.
  • Having rules in someone’s head that nobody else knows.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The team agrees and writes down its safety routine before opening, so every member does it the same way.
  • One person is responsible for checking the routine has been followed before service starts.
  • If something is missed, the team has an agreed way to fix it rather than carrying on.

3 What I need to know
Visual · Ready to Cook Checklist
🧼Clean hands
🨍Clean apron
👳Hair tied back
🩹Cuts covered — blue plaster
💍No jewellery
📱Phone away
✅ Must know
  • Ready to cook = clean hands, clean apron, hair tied back, cuts covered with a blue plaster, no jewellery, phone away.
  • A blue plaster is used because blue shows up clearly if it falls into food.
  • Anyone who feels unwell — sickness, diarrhoea, a heavy cold — should not handle food, because illness passes to customers through food.
  • Standards slip when people rush or arrive at different times, so professionals build a check, not a hope.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Assuming everyone prepared properly instead of checking.
  • Letting someone start because they’re in a hurry.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The team runs a quick “ready to cook” check together at the start of every shift.
  • One person keeps spare aprons, hair ties and blue plasters stocked at the station.
  • There’s an agreed rule: if you feel unwell, you tell the team and take a non-food job.

Stop Invisible Enemies

“How does my team stop germs spreading during a busy service?”
🧠 Smart about Knowledge
1 The situation
During a practice run, your team washes hands perfectly at the start — but two hours into a busy service, hands haven’t been washed in a while and people are touching phones, money and food. How do you keep hygiene going when it’s busy?

After you watch — Restaurant Reflection: When in a busy service is your team most likely to forget to wash hands?
3 What I need to know
Visual · The Invisible Journey
📱Phone→✋Hand→🍴Knife→🍽Food→🤗Customer
1Wet
2Soap
3Scrub 20s
4Between fingers
5Rinse
6Dry
✅ Must know
  • Germs are too small to see and spread easily on hands, equipment and surfaces.
  • Wash for about 20 seconds with soap — palms, backs, between fingers, under nails.
  • Wash before handling food, after the toilet, after touching raw meat, after handling money or a phone, and after coughing or touching your face.
  • Hand washing fails not because people don’t know how, but because nobody builds it into the busy moments — so professionals plan the wash points.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Washing well at the start, then forgetting once it’s busy.
  • Handling money and food without washing in between.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The team agrees its “wash points” — the exact moments everyone washes — and posts them at the station.
  • Soap and clean towels are stocked and checked before service.
  • Where possible, one person handles money and another handles food, to break the germ route.

Control the Kitchen

“How does my team design a kitchen that prevents cross-contamination?”
🧠 Smart about Thinking
1 The situation
Your menu has both raw chicken and a fresh salad. Your stall has one small table and two chopping boards. How do you set up the space and the order of work so raw and ready-to-eat food never meet?
2 Professional training video
Before you watch: Watch for how germs travel from one food to another.
▶Watch: Avoiding cross-contaminationopens in a new tab
While you watch, look for:
  • how germs move from raw food
  • why we keep raw and ready-to-eat food apart
  • how to use chopping boards safely
After you watch — Restaurant Reflection: Where in your team’s menu could raw and ready-to-eat food meet?
3 What I need to know
Visual · The Contamination Chain
🦡Sourceraw meat→✋Transferhands & boards→🍳Food→🤗Customer
✅ Must know
  • Cross-contamination is when germs move from one food to another, usually from raw meat, fish and eggs.
  • Keep raw and ready-to-eat apart: separate boards and utensils, wash hands between, clean surfaces, store raw at the bottom of the fridge.
  • Colour-coding boards (e.g. red for raw meat, green for salad) makes mistakes far less likely.
  • In a small space, the order and flow of work matters as much as the equipment — prep ready-to-eat before raw, or keep them on opposite sides.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Using one board for everything because space is tight.
  • Prepping salad right after raw chicken on the same surface.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The team assigns boards and utensils to raw vs ready-to-eat and labels them.
  • The team plans the order and layout of prep so raw and ready-to-eat are separated in space or time.
  • Surfaces are cleaned on an agreed schedule between tasks.

Protect the Ingredients

“How does my team keep food at safe temperatures all through service?”
🧠 Smart about Situations
1 The situation
Your team preps food in the morning, but you don’t open until midday, and the kitchen is warm. Some dishes need to stay cold, some need to stay hot. How do you keep everything safe from prep to serving — for hours?
2 Professional training video
Before you watch: Watch for what happens to food when it is kept warm.
▶Watch: Storing food safelyopens in a new tab▶Watch: Holding food at safe temperaturesopens in a new tab
While you watch, look for:
  • why temperature matters
  • where different foods should be kept
  • what happens when food is left out
After you watch — Restaurant Reflection: Which food on your team’s menu would be most at risk if it was left out?
3 What I need to know
Visual · The Temperature Danger Zone
Hot & safe — above 63°Ckeep hot food hot
DANGER ZONE — 5°C to 63°Cgerms grow fast — don’t leave food here
Cold & safe — below 5°Ckeep cold food in the fridge
Safe Storage
✅ Top shelves: ready-to-eat food — cooked food, salad, desserts
⚠️ Bottom shelf: raw meat, fish and eggs
✅ Must know
  • Germs grow fastest in the danger zone, 5°C to 63°C — keep cold food below 5°C and hot food above 63°C.
  • Don’t leave food sitting out; store it correctly until it’s needed, and keep it covered.
  • Store raw food at the bottom of the fridge so it can’t drip onto ready-to-eat food.
  • Food prepped hours ahead needs a plan — what goes in the fridge, what’s kept hot, and when it’s brought out.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Leaving prepped food out on the counter, especially in a warm room.
  • Bringing all the food out at opening and letting it sit for hours.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The team decides where each dish is stored and keeps it covered and labelled.
  • The team brings food out in batches as needed, rather than all at once.
  • Someone is responsible for checking food goes back into storage after prep.

Serve Everyone Safely

“How does my team manage allergens so every customer is safe?”
🧠 Smart about People
1 The situation
Customers will ask what’s in your food. Your dishes have several ingredients, and different people made them. How does your team make sure you can always give a correct, instant answer about allergens — and never guess?

While you watch, look for:

3 What I need to know
Visual · The Allergen Safety System
🗣️Customer
asks→📋Check the
Allergen Card→🤷Not sure?ask an adult→✅Tell the
customer
MilkEggsFishShellfishPeanutsTree nutsWheatSoyaSesame
✅ Must know
  • An allergy can make someone seriously ill, sometimes from a tiny trace — this is not a preference, it’s a medical risk.
  • Common allergens: milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, peanuts, tree nuts, wheat, soya, sesame.
  • Cross-contact matters: an allergen can transfer via shared utensils, surfaces or hands, even if it’s not an ingredient.
  • The team keeps an accurate Allergen Card for every dish and never answers from memory; no peanuts or tree nuts are used in the food court.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Guessing or saying “I think it’s fine” about an allergen.
  • Forgetting that an allergen can transfer through shared equipment, not just ingredients.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The team writes an Allergen Card listing every ingredient in each dish, and keeps it visible.
  • The team prevents cross-contact by using clean utensils and surfaces when preparing for an allergy request.
  • There’s an agreed script: “Let me check the card for you,” and ask an adult if unsure.

Pass the Inspection

“How does my team stay inspection-ready all through service?”
🧠 Smart about Situations
1 The situation
At the start, your stall is spotless. But after an hour of service there are spills, a full bin and clutter building up. How does your team stay clean and safe right through the busiest part of the day — not just at opening?
2 Professional training video
Before you watch: Watch for how professionals keep a kitchen clean during a rush.
▶Watch: Cleaning & sanitising equipmentopens in a new tab▶Watch: Keeping the kitchen cleanopens in a new tab
While you watch, look for:
  • how professionals keep a kitchen clean
  • hazards to watch out for
  • why we clean as we go
After you watch — Restaurant Reflection: What is one cleaning habit your team must keep during a busy service?
3 What I need to know
Visual · The Opening Inspection Cycle
🧹Prepare→🔍Check→🍽Serve→📝Review↻
✅ Must know
  • Clean as you go — wipe surfaces between tasks and clear spills immediately.
  • Watch for hazards: wet floors are slip risks, pans get hot, knives are carried point-down and never left in sinks.
  • Keep the bin away from food and emptied before it overflows; do a full clean after service.
  • Staying clean during a rush needs a plan and shared jobs — it won’t happen by accident when everyone’s busy.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Leaving cleaning until the end instead of cleaning as you go.
  • Letting the bin overflow or spills sit during the rush.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The team has a cleaning rota — who cleans what, and when, during service.
  • Spills and hazards are dealt with immediately by whoever is nearest.
  • The team agrees the end-of-service clean before they start.
