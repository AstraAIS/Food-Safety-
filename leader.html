Protect the Customer

“How do I create a culture where safety happens even when nobody is watching?”
🧠 Smart about People
1 The situation
It’s the busiest moment of the day and you’re leading the team. You notice a younger teammate skip a safety step because they’re rushing — and no customer or teacher saw it. What do you do, and what does it say about the culture you’ve built?
2 Professional training video

▶Watch: Why food safety mattersopens in a new tab
While you watch, look for:
  • why food safety matters
  • what customers are trusting you to do
  • that food safety is everyone’s job
After you watch — Restaurant Reflection: What is one thing from this video your team must remember on Opening Day?
3 What I need to know
Visual · The Customer Trust Chain
🧑Me→🍽Food→🤗Customer→⭐Trust
✅ Must know
  • Customers trust that food is safe, the kitchen is clean and you’ve been honest — and they’re trusting your leadership, not just your cooking.
  • A safe restaurant runs on culture: the team does the right thing when no one is checking.
  • Culture is set by what a leader notices, praises and corrects — not by what they say once.
  • When something goes wrong, how the leader responds teaches the team more than any rule.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Only enforcing standards when a teacher or customer is watching.
  • Letting a small slip pass to avoid an awkward moment — which quietly lowers the standard for everyone.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The leader sets the standard out loud, models it, and checks it calmly and consistently.
  • Mistakes are treated as something to fix and learn from, not to hide — so people speak up early.
  • The leader coaches teammates who slip, rather than blaming them, so the standard sticks.

Visual · Ready to Cook Checklist
🧼Clean hands
🨍Clean apron
👳Hair tied back
🩹Cuts covered — blue plaster
💍No jewellery

✅ Must know
  • Ready to cook = clean hands, apron, hair tied, cuts covered (blue plaster), no jewellery, phone away — and not handling food when unwell.
  • The danger of hygiene shortcuts is invisible: nothing looks wrong until someone gets ill.
  • A leader’s hardest job is holding the standard with people who are confident it doesn’t matter.
  • Teams copy the most influential person, so leaders address the influential corner-cutter, not just the rule.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Avoiding the awkward conversation because the person is popular or confident.
  • Repeating the rule to everyone instead of addressing the individual setting the example.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The leader holds the standard consistently, including with confident teammates.
  • The leader explains the “why” (invisible risk) so the standard makes sense, not just “because I said”.
  • The leader praises good practice publicly and corrects slips privately.

Stop Invisible Enemies

“How do I lead hygiene so it holds without me policing it?”
🧠 Smart about Knowledge
1 The situation
You can’t watch everyone’s hands all service. As the leader, how do you build hand hygiene into the team so it happens automatically — and what do you do when you spot it slipping two hours in?

3 What I need to know
Visual · The Invisible Journey
📱Phone→✋Hand→🍴Knife→🍽Food→🤗Customer
1Wet
2Soap
3Scrub 20s
4Between fingers
5Rinse

✅ Must know
  • Germs spread invisibly via hands, surfaces and equipment; a 20-second wash with soap at the key moments removes them.
  • Wash before food, after the toilet, after raw meat, after money or a phone, after coughing or touching the face.
  • A leader can’t personally police every wash, so they build habits and shared accountability instead.
  • The moment a leader ignores one slip, the team learns the standard is optional.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Trying to personally watch every hand wash instead of building habit and peer accountability.
  • Ignoring the first slip, which quietly resets the standard.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The leader builds wash points into the routine and gets the team to remind each other.
  • The leader does quick, calm spot-checks and praises good habits.
  • Anyone — including juniors — is allowed to remind anyone, including the leader, to wash.

Control the Kitchen

“How do I lead a team that controls contamination under pressure?”
🧠 Smart about Thinking
1 The situation
Your team designed a good raw / ready-to-eat system. But during a busy rush you see it breaking down — boards getting mixed up, people grabbing the nearest knife. As the leader, how do you hold the system when it’s under pressure?
2 Professional training video
Before you watch: Watch for how germs travel from one food to another.
▶Watch: Avoiding cross-contaminationopens in a new tab
While you watch, look for:
  • how germs move from raw food
  • why we keep raw and ready-to-eat food apart
  • how to use chopping boards safely
After you watch — Restaurant Reflection: Where in your team’s menu could raw and ready-to-eat food meet?
3 What I need to know
Visual · The Contamination Chain
🦡Sourceraw meat→✋Transferhands & boards→🍳Food→🤗Customer
✅ Must know
  • Cross-contamination moves germs from raw meat, fish and eggs to ready-to-eat food; it’s prevented by separation, cleaning and hand-washing.
  • Colour-coded boards, planned flow and storing raw at the bottom all reduce risk.
  • Good systems fail under pressure if no one is responsible for holding them — so a leader assigns ownership.
  • The leader’s job in a rush is to keep the system intact, not to abandon it to go faster.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Letting the system collapse “just for the rush” to keep up with the queue.
  • Not assigning anyone to own contamination control, so everyone assumes someone else has it.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The leader assigns clear ownership of raw vs ready-to-eat stations.
  • The leader watches the pressure points and steps in early when the system starts slipping.
  • After service, the leader reviews what broke down and improves the layout for next time.

Protect the Ingredients

“How do I lead a team that protects ingredients without being reminded?”
🧠 Smart about Situations
1 The situation
Your team knows the temperature rules. But you notice that when you’re not watching, prepped food gets left out “just for a minute” that turns into twenty. As leader, how do you build temperature discipline into the team?
2 Professional training video
Before you watch: Watch for what happens to food when it is kept warm.
▶Watch: Storing food safelyopens in a new tab▶Watch: Holding food at safe temperaturesopens in a new tab
While you watch, look for:
  • why temperature matters
  • where different foods should be kept
  • what happens when food is left out
After you watch — Restaurant Reflection: Which food on your team’s menu would be most at risk if it was left out?
3 What I need to know
Visual · The Temperature Danger Zone
Hot & safe — above 63°Ckeep hot food hot
DANGER ZONE — 5°C to 63°Cgerms grow fast — don’t leave food here
Cold & safe — below 5°Ckeep cold food in the fridge
Safe Storage
✅ Top shelves: ready-to-eat food — cooked food, salad, desserts
⚠️ Bottom shelf: raw meat, fish and eggs
✅ Must know
  • The danger zone is 5–63°C; cold stays below 5°C, hot above 63°C, and food shouldn’t sit out.
  • Raw at the bottom of the fridge, everything covered and labelled.
  • Temperature risk is invisible and gradual — food left out looks fine long after it’s become unsafe, which is why discipline matters.
  • A leader builds ownership and quick checks rather than relying on constant supervision.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Assuming people will put food away without an owner or a check.
  • Treating “just for a minute” as harmless because the food still looks fine.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The leader assigns who owns storage and gives the team a quick self-check (“is anything sitting out?”).
  • The leader does brief temperature and storage spot-checks during service.
  • The leader reviews any near-misses and tightens the routine.

Serve Everyone Safely

“How do I lead a team that never gets allergens wrong?”
🧠 Smart about People
1 The situation
Allergens are the highest-stakes part of your restaurant — a mistake could seriously harm someone. As the leader, how do you make sure your whole team handles allergen questions perfectly, including the youngest members, even when it’s busy?

3 What I need to know
Visual · The Allergen Safety System
🗣️Customer
asks→📋Check the
Allergen Card→🤷Not sure?ask an adult→✅Tell the
customer
MilkEggsFishShellfishPeanutsTree nutsWheatSoyaSesame
✅ Must know
  • An allergic reaction can be severe and fast; allergens are the highest-risk area of food safety.
  • Common allergens: milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, peanuts, tree nuts, wheat, soya, sesame; cross-contact through equipment counts too.
  • The safest answer when unsure is always to pause and check — a leader makes “never guess” an absolute team rule.
  • A leader makes sure even the youngest or shyest member knows exactly what to do and feels able to say “let me check.”
✗ Common mistakes
  • Assuming everyone will handle an allergy question correctly without checking.
  • Letting a busy moment pressure the team into a quick guess.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The leader makes “never guess — always check the card and ask an adult” a non-negotiable team rule.
  • The leader checks that every team member, including juniors, can confidently use the Allergen Card.
  • The leader sets up clean tools and space for allergy orders and reviews allergen handling after service.

“How do I lead a team that’s always ready for inspection?”
🧠 Smart about Situations
1 The situation
An inspection — or a teacher, or a customer — could look at your stall at any moment. As the leader, how do you make sure your team keeps the place clean and safe throughout, and how do you do a final check before you open?
2 Professional training video
Before you watch: Watch for how professionals keep a kitchen clean during a rush.
▶Watch: Cleaning & sanitising equipmentopens in a new tab▶Watch: Keeping the kitchen cleanopens in a new tab
While you watch, look for:
  • how professionals keep a kitchen clean
  • hazards to watch out for
  • why we clean as we go
After you watch — Restaurant Reflection: What is one cleaning habit your team must keep during a busy service?
3 What I need to know
Visual · The Opening Inspection Cycle
🧹Prepare→🔍Check→🍽Serve→📝Review↻
✅ Must know
  • Clean-as-you-go, immediate hazard control, bin management and a full after-service clean keep a stall inspection-ready.
  • Hazards (wet floors, hot pans, knives) must be controlled continuously, not just noticed.
  • A leader runs a final readiness check before opening and keeps standards up during service through shared ownership.
  • Inspection-readiness reflects the whole culture — a team that cleans when no one’s watching is a team that’s led well.
✗ Common mistakes
  • Doing a big clean only at the start and assuming it lasts.
  • Not running a final check before opening, so problems are found by a customer instead of the team.
🔨 Restaurant systems
  • The leader runs an opening readiness check (Prepare → Check → Serve → Review) before trading.
  • The leader assigns cleaning ownership and keeps it going during the rush.
  • The leader reviews after service and improves the routine for next time.
